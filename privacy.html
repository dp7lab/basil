Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 September 2026

In short: Basil has no accounts and no servers. Everything you add — item names, dates, photos, notes — stays on your phone.

Basil sends data over the internet in one situation only: when you scan a product barcode, the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts to look up the product name. Nothing else about you is sent.

Who we are

Basil is developed by DP7 Lab. You can reach us at dp7lab.support@gmail.com with any question about this policy or your data.

What Basil stores on your device

Basil keeps the following on your phone, in the app's private storage:

  • Item names, expiry dates, quantities, storage locations, and notes you enter
  • Photos you choose to attach to an item
  • Your record of what you ate and what you threw away, used to build the statistics screen
  • Your settings: reminder time, theme, language, and the food categories and locations you create

We have no server and no account system, so we cannot see any of this. It is not uploaded, synced, or backed up to us. Uninstalling Basil deletes all of it from your device.

What Basil sends over the internet

Basil makes network requests in one case: barcode lookup.

When you scan a product barcode, Basil sends the barcode number only to the Open Food Facts database at world.openfoodfacts.org to retrieve the product name and product image. If a product image is found and shown, your device also downloads that image from Open Food Facts.

As with any request to any website, Open Food Facts can see your device's IP address. Basil does not send your name, email, device identifier, location, or any of the items already saved in your app.

Open Food Facts is an independent non-profit project. Their handling of requests is governed by their own privacy policy at world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy.

If you never scan a barcode, Basil makes no network requests at all. The rest of the app works fully offline.

Camera and photo scanning

Basil asks for camera permission so it can read barcodes and read expiry dates printed on packaging.

This reading happens entirely on your device, using Google's ML Kit libraries bundled inside the app. The camera image is analysed in memory and discarded. Camera frames are never uploaded anywhere, including to us and to Google.

Photos you deliberately attach to an item are saved in the app's private storage on your phone and are not uploaded.

Notifications

Basil asks for notification permission so it can remind you before food expires. Reminders are generated on your device from data already stored there. There is no push server, and no notification content leaves your phone.

Backups you create

Basil lets you export your data to a file. That file is written wherever you choose to save it, and from that point it is under your control, not ours. If you place it in a cloud folder, that cloud provider's policy applies to it. We never receive a copy.

Purchases

Basil offers an optional paid upgrade. If you buy it, the payment is processed by Google Play. We never see or store your card number or billing details. We receive only the confirmation needed to unlock the paid features on your device.

Analytics, advertising and tracking

Basil contains no advertising, no analytics, and no crash reporting. We do not track your usage, we do not build a profile of you, and we do not share or sell data to anyone. There is nothing to opt out of, because nothing is collected.

Your rights

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, the GDPR gives you rights over your personal data, including access, correction, deletion, and portability.

In practice these rights are already in your hands: your data lives only on your device. You can view and edit it in the app, export it to a file at any time, and delete all of it by uninstalling Basil or clearing the app's storage in Android settings. We hold no copy to give you, correct, or erase.

The one exception is the barcode lookup described above. If you would prefer that no barcode ever leaves your device, do not use the barcode scanner. You can add items by typing them or by scanning only the expiry date.

Children

Basil is a general-purpose household tool and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If Basil changes in a way that affects this policy, we will update this page and change the date at the top. Significant changes will also be noted in the app's release notes on Google Play.
